# Hyperlocal GTM Playbook

Hyperlocal consumer products fail for one reason 90% of the time:
they tried to grow before they were dense in **one** neighborhood. The
playbook below treats density as the only metric that matters until
proven, then layers distribution.

## Step 0 — Pick the wedge geography (not the TAM)

Resist "all of [country]" or even "all of [city]". Pick the smallest
unit where density makes the product feel alive: a neighborhood
(Zindabazar), a campus (Shahjalal University), a community
(Sylheti diaspora in East London).

Density target before expansion: the product should feel useful to a
random visitor without you handholding the supply side.

## Step 1 — Break the chicken-and-egg (supply first, almost always)

For directories / marketplaces: **seed the supply manually**. Do not
launch with empty categories.

- Scrape, walk, or call to build the first 500-2000 listings yourself
- Verify a meaningful subset by hand (badge them)
- Do **not** offer self-serve listing until supply has critical mass —
  otherwise the first impression is "this product is empty"
- The work is unsexy and not scalable. That's the point — competitors
  who want a clever growth hack won't do it.

For events, blog, content: seed with **owned production** (you write,
you organize) for ~6 months before opening to community contribution.

## Step 2 — Demand-side distribution channels (in priority order)

Pick at most **3** for the first 12 months. Cycling channels = none of
them get to flywheel.

| Channel | When it works | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing community watering holes** (Facebook groups, WhatsApp, subreddit) | When you're a credible community member, not an ad | Drive-by promotion, no give-before-take |
| **Diaspora pull** (people abroad recommend to family at home) | High-trust use cases (healthcare, real estate, services for visiting relatives) | Ignoring the diaspora as a persona |
| **Religious / civic institution partnerships** (mosque committee, market association) | When the institution has list authority and reputation to lend | Treating it as advertising not partnership |
| **University ambassador program** | Younger demographic, longer time horizon, low-budget | No reason for the student to stay engaged |
| **SEO** (long-tail local queries in local language) | When you have unique content competitors lack | English-only content, generic listings |
| **Paid ads** | Last resort once organic loops are proven | Burning budget before product-market fit |
| **PR / journalism** | One-time burst for credibility | Treating it as a growth channel |

Anti-channels (avoid in year 1): influencer paid posts, SEO black-hat
listing-stuffing, billboards, generic Google Ads. They look like
marketing; they don't move the metric.

## Step 3 — Trust scaffolding (the hidden GTM)

In emerging markets and small cities, trust is THE conversion barrier.
Pick 2-3 trust signals visible on every page from day one:

- **Human verification badge** (with method visible: "verified by phone
  + photo of storefront")
- **Last-verified date** (decays without re-check)
- **Community moderator names** (real people from this city)
- **Reviewer accountability** (ID-tied or community-known, not anonymous
  Gmail accounts)
- **Source transparency** (where did this listing come from?)

Trust is not a feature — it is the product, for hyperlocal directories.

## Step 4 — Staged metric ladder

Do not chase MAU early. Stages, in order:

1. **Density** — % of relevant local businesses listed in the wedge
   geography (target: >70% before public launch)
2. **Verification rate** — % of listings with current owner contact
3. **First-visit usefulness** — random user finds answer to a real
   query within 90 seconds (test weekly with 5 strangers)
4. **Repeat use** — returning visitors / week
5. **Owner claim rate** — % of listings claimed by actual owners
   (signals trust + utility from supply side)
6. **Earned referral** — % of new users from word-of-mouth (the only
   sustainable channel)

Only after #6 trends positive should paid acquisition or expansion to
the next geography start.

## Step 5 — Revenue, deliberately late

Resist monetizing in year 1. Every revenue mechanism trades off trust:

- **Paid featured listings** → buyer can't tell paid from earned →
  reviews lose credibility (Yelp's failure mode)
- **Sponsored content** → editorial trust erosion
- **Owner-paid claim/management SaaS** → safer (aligned incentive)
- **Event ticketing fees** → safe, owner-paid
- **B2B data / API** → safe, doesn't touch consumer trust
- **Diaspora premium services** (concierge for visiting relatives) →
  unique to this category, high-margin

Pick **one** revenue line, late. Diversify only after it's stable.

## Output

A GTM brief with:

1. Wedge geography (with rationale)
2. Supply seeding plan (with target listings and weeks)
3. 3 chosen demand channels (with first 90 days of specific activity)
4. Trust scaffolding spec (with the 2-3 signals on every page)
5. Metric ladder with target thresholds per stage
6. Year-1 revenue: one line, with the trust tradeoff acknowledged

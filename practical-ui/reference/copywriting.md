# Copywriting (interface text)

## Be concise
Cut any word that can go without losing meaning.
- Drop fillers (actually, basically, really, quite), many articles (a/an/the) in labels, and intro phrases ("Would you like to", "In order to", "Are you sure", "There are", "It is").
- Prefer short words; sentences under ~20 words.
- e.g. a wordy "Would you like to save the article?…Yes, save the article" dialog → "Save article?" / "Save the article to your library to publish later" / [Save article] [Cancel].

## Sentence case
Capitalise only the first word and proper nouns. Title Case is inconsistent and slows scanning.

## Plain language
Write for a 12-year-old new to the topic. No jargon, no slang ("bee's knees", "Beam me up!"), short words, natural contractions (you're, they're).

## Front-load
Key information/benefit first in headings, list items, and links: "30% off if you sign up today", "Learn UI design by subscribing…", "5 UI design eBooks you should read".

## Inverted pyramid
Most important info in the heading (enough to act on), supporting detail beneath, background details behind a link/another screen.

## Abbreviations
Avoid; if used, expand on first use. "Apartment number", not "Apt. no.".

## UPPERCASE
Avoid for sentences (all words become same-shaped rectangles). OK for short eyebrow labels: small, bold, wider letter spacing.

## Structure
Break content into chunks with **descriptive headings** and bullets. Headings must convey the point ("Free secure parking", not "Parking") — scanners and screen-reader heading lists depend on them.

## Form labels
No "my" (ambiguous — the interface is talking) and usually no "your": just "Email".

## Consistent vocabulary
One word per concept across the product. Watch: cart/bag, publish/post, sign up/register, log in/sign in, delete/remove, subscribe/join.

## Numbers
Numerals, not words ("899 designers"). Thousands separators (2,420). Abbreviate huge numbers ("1 billion", "12K").

## Punctuation
No full stops on short UI text (headings, labels, single-sentence descriptions) unless it's a multi-clause sentence. Be consistent across sibling elements.

## Even lengths
Sibling text blocks in a row (feature columns, cards) should be similar length so they align.

## Links
Describe the destination: "Explore templates", "Email marketing features", not "Learn more" ×3 or "Click here". Alternatively make the heading the link. Never "click" (not everyone uses a mouse).

## Error messages (see forms.md)
Say what happened, why, and how to fix it. Never blame the user; skip "Oops/sorry/please"; human not robotic; descriptive heading + button ("Payment failed" / "Update your payment details and try again" / [Update payment details]).

## Button text (see buttons.md)
Verb + noun: "Save post", "Discard message", "Edit article". Never "OK"/"Yes".
